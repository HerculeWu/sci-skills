# A worked example: star-cluster dynamics, 2026
One earlier calibration, on a library of 44 papers on the tidal tails of star clusters (29 mixed observation-and-model, 11 observation, 4 simulation; 42 with full texts). It is here to borrow from where it fits another field, and to show the level of detail that proved useful. Its lists are this field's, not a standard.

## Kinds it used
Labelled by four readers over the Introductions and closing sections of the 19 simulation and theory papers; Methods and Results were read from section titles.

| Section | Kinds |
|---|---|
| Introduction | STAKE (why it matters), PHYSICS (how the object works, in the authors' voice), OBS (what is measured, how far, how precisely), MODELS (earlier models and what they found), TOOLS, OBJECT, CONFLICT (named works disagree), GAP, AIM, ANSWER (the result, stated early), OUTLINE |
| Methods | CODE, POTENTIAL (the Galactic potential and orbit), MODELS (initial conditions), QUANTITY (a measure defined), DATA (catalogue, selection, mock observer) |
| Results | CASE (a representative model worked through), GENERAL, RIVAL (an alternative explanation weighed), NULL |
| Closing | SUMMARY, MECHANISM, COMPARISON, LIMITATION, IMPLICATION, FUTURE |

POTENTIAL is this field's; another field would have its own setup kind, or fold it into a broader one.

## What the section counts looked like
- Whole paper (40 papers): 6 sections, 9 subsections, about 8000 words, 67 paragraphs of about 100 words, 2–5 paragraphs per heading, abstract 275 words.
- Introduction (19 papers): median 7 paragraphs; opens with PHYSICS or STAKE in 15; MODELS present in 17; ANSWER in 4, never with numbers; own code named in 0; OUTLINE last in 17, never cited.
- Closing (19 papers): a separate Discussion in 6; Conclusions in prose in 15; numbers repeated in 17; opens with SUMMARY; ends with FUTURE in 10.
- Abstract (41): median 9 numbers; 18 open with "We present / investigate", 23 with a statement about the world.

Counts like "4 of 19 state the result in the Introduction, never with numbers" were the useful kind: each settles a question the writer would otherwise decide by habit.

## Metrics it measured
Per sentence unless stated; median (IQR) over 42 full texts, and the agent-written draft the author complained about.

| Metric | Library | Draft |
|---|---|---|
| words per sentence | 25 (24–27) | 32 |
| sentences ≥ 35 words | 18 % (14–24) | 40 % |
| opening with a connective, demonstrative or pronoun | 28 % (22–32) | 8.8 % |
| with an epistemic hedge | 6.8 % (4.2–8.2) | 0.6 % |
| "X, not Y" corrections, per 1000 words | 0.3 (0.2–0.5) | 1.4 |
| with "we" or "our" | 25 % (16–33) | 16 % |
| with a citation | 15 % (13–20) | 20 % |
| containing a passive | 28 % (25–34) | — |
| it / its, per 1000 words | 3.6 (2.8–4.6) | 15.0 |
| wh-clause where the field writes a noun phrase, per 1000 words | 1.5 (0.8–2.0) | 4.6 |
| colon introducing a clause, per 1000 words | 1.0 (0.7–1.8) | 3.8 |

Later the project added the field's own words (its verbs for gravity, "more massive" for "heavier"); such lists are found by comparing drafts with the library, which is `write-paper`'s review, not this calibration.

## How it was found, and what it taught
- **The author's complaints were tested before they became rules.** Five complaints about a draft were measured on the library: three held and were extreme, one did not (a term the author disliked was the field's own word). A complaint is a lead; the library decides.
- **Measured layers moved; written ones did not.** The register came into range the first time it was measured; rules that were only written ("say how each sentence follows from the one before") failed in every version until measured.
- **Every rule was read to its extreme.** "Use fewer *thus*" dropped it to a fifth of the field's rate; a one-sided range read as a ban drove "we" too low; naming a target rate made the writer write for the rate. Hence two-sided ranges, and ranges stated as testimony.
- **The map that was used was the one that pointed at paragraphs.** The first library map was a keyword scan of the Shorts (code names, potentials, surveys), which only said where to start looking. A map from kinds to numbered paragraphs lets the writer open the exact paragraph to copy.
